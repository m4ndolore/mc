# DoD SBIR/STTR proposals

DoD SBIR and STTR proposals are submitted through the Defense SBIR/STTR Innovation Portal (DSIP, dodsbirsttr.mil). Each component (Army, Navy, Air Force and Space Force, DLA, MDA, SOCOM and others) publishes its own instructions on top of the DoD-wide solicitation. Read both. Limits on pages, cost and period of performance differ by component and by topic.

## Volumes

A DoD SBIR/STTR proposal in DSIP is typically made of these volumes. Confirm the list in the current solicitation, because volumes are added and renumbered:

| Volume | Contents | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Proposal cover sheet | Entered in DSIP: company data, topic, title, technical abstract, anticipated benefits, key words | The abstract may be released publicly if the award is made. Keep it free of proprietary data. |
| 2. Technical volume | The proposal itself (see below) | Page or slide limit set by the component. Pages over the limit are not read, or the proposal is rejected. |
| 3. Cost volume | Entered in DSIP: labor, materials, travel, subcontracts, indirect rates, fee | Use `price-to-win`. STTR: show the research institution's share. |
| 4. Company commercialization report | Prior SBIR/STTR awards and their outcomes, maintained in the SBA company registry | Companies with prior awards are judged on their transition record. |
| 5. Supporting documents | Letters of support, resumes or technical data the component allows | Some components do not read these. Check before relying on one. |
| 6. Fraud, waste and abuse training | Certificate of completion from DSIP | Required by DoD; done once per proposal cycle. |
| 7. Foreign affiliations disclosure | Disclosures about foreign relationships of the company and key personnel | Added for SBIR/STTR due diligence. Answer completely; an omission can disqualify an award. |

## Phase I technical volume

DoD instructions commonly ask for these elements. Use the component's order and names if they differ:

1. **Identification and significance of the problem or opportunity.** The topic's problem in the government's words, and why it matters to the warfighter or mission.
2. **Phase I technical objectives.** What Phase I will prove. Feasibility, not the finished system.
3. **Phase I statement of work.** Tasks, who does each (STTR: which tasks the research institution does), schedule and deliverables. Mark which tasks are the small business's and which are subcontracted.
4. **Related work.** The team's prior work that this builds on, and others' work that it improves on.
5. **Relationship with future research and development.** What Phase II would build, and the path to a fielded capability.
6. **Commercialization strategy.** The government customer and transition path (program office, program of record, follow-on vehicle), plus commercial markets. Evaluators look for a named customer.
7. **Key personnel.** Principal investigator and key staff, with the experience the topic needs. SBIR: the PI's primary employment must be with the small business during the award.
8. **Foreign citizens.** Any foreign nationals who will work on the project, as the instructions require.
9. **Facilities and equipment.** What the team has, and that it meets any security or safety requirement.
10. **Subcontractors and consultants.** Who, for what, and their share of the work.
11. **Prior, current or pending support.** Any similar proposal or award to any agency. Duplicate funding is not allowed; disclose overlap.

## Phase II

Phase II proposals are usually by invitation or limited to Phase I awardees, though direct-to-Phase-II topics exist. Expect a larger technical volume, a detailed transition and commercialization plan, a test and evaluation plan, and stronger evidence from Phase I results. Read the Phase II instructions for the component and topic; do not reuse a Phase I outline unchanged.

## Before submission

- SAM.gov registration active, with the UEI on the proposal.
- SBA company registry entry, and its SBC control ID.
- DSIP account for the company and for each person who certifies.
- Topic Q&A: during pre-release, the topic author (TPOC) can be contacted directly. After the topic opens, questions go only through the DSIP topic Q&A.
- Submit early. DSIP closes at the stated time and late proposals are not accepted.
