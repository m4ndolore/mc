# CSOs, BAAs and innovation-unit challenges

These calls are shorter and more commercial than a FAR proposal, and usually staged: a short first submission, then a pitch or full proposal by invitation. Each call sets its own format. Use these outlines only where the call is silent.

## DIU Commercial Solutions Opening

DIU solicits through Commercial Solutions Openings that lead to other transaction prototype agreements. The process is usually:

1. **Solution brief.** A short submission, either a few pages or a slide deck, as the call specifies. It is screened against the problem statement.
2. **Pitch.** Selected companies present to the government team, often within weeks.
3. **Prototype proposal.** Selected companies submit a proposal and negotiate an agreement.

Solution brief outline:

1. **The problem, in DIU's words,** and the outcome the users need.
2. **The solution** as it exists today: what is built, what it has done, and its maturity. Commercial traction counts.
3. **How it meets each requirement** in the call, in the call's order.
4. **Prototype plan:** what would be delivered, demonstrated and tested, on what timeline.
5. **Company:** team, funding, customers, relevant past performance.
6. **Rough price and schedule,** if the call asks for them. Use `price-to-win` for a range.

DIU awards often run through other transaction consortia or direct agreements, which can affect who appears as the awardee in public data.

## Broad agency announcements

DARPA and other agencies use broad agency announcements for research. Many encourage an **abstract** or **white paper** before a full proposal, and give feedback on whether to submit one.

Abstract or white paper outline:

1. **Concept summary:** the idea and what it would achieve, in a few sentences.
2. **Innovation:** what is new compared to the state of the art, and why it could work.
3. **Technical approach:** how the team will do it, and the main risks.
4. **Team:** who, and why they can do it.
5. **Rough cost and schedule.**

Full BAA proposals usually have a technical volume and a cost volume with the structure the BAA sets out. Read the BAA's evaluation criteria and the program's goals and metrics; programs often state quantitative targets the proposal must address.

## Innovation-unit challenges

AFWERX, SOFWERX, Army xTech, NavalX and hubs listed on Ratio Exchange run challenges, pitch competitions and assessment events. Submissions are often a short form, a slide deck or a video.

Pitch deck outline (adjust to the slide limit):

1. The user's problem, in the challenge's words.
2. The solution and a demonstration or evidence that it works.
3. How it meets the challenge criteria.
4. Maturity, transition path and the government customer.
5. Team and traction.
6. The ask: what the company wants from the award (funding, testing access, a follow-on).

Prize challenges and assessment events can lead to other transaction agreements or SBIR awards. Read the rules for intellectual property, data rights and eligibility before submitting.
