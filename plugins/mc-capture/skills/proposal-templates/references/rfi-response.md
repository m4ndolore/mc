# Sources sought, RFIs and capability statements

A sources sought notice or request for information is market research. The government is deciding whether to buy, how to buy, and whether to set the work aside for small businesses. A good response can shape the requirement and the set-aside before a solicitation exists. Responses are not proposals and are not evaluated for award.

## Response outline

Follow the notice's questions and order exactly when it gives them. Where it does not:

1. **Cover:** company name, UEI, CAGE code, address, point of contact, business size and socioeconomic status (small business, 8(a), HUBZone, SDVOSB, WOSB), and NAICS codes. Answer the notice's NAICS size question directly.
2. **Understanding of the requirement:** two or three sentences in the notice's own terms.
3. **Capability:** how the company would do this work, with evidence (deployments, tests, customers).
4. **Relevant past performance:** contract or project, customer, value, period, and what was delivered. Use `market-research` to confirm contract numbers from public award data.
5. **Answers to the notice's questions,** numbered to match.
6. **Recommendations:** suggestions on the acquisition approach, such as contract type, set-aside, schedule or requirement wording, if the notice invites them. This is where a response shapes the solicitation.

Keep within any page limit, and send it to the address and by the date the notice gives.

## Capability statement

A one- or two-page summary a company hands to program offices and primes:

| Section | Contents |
|---|---|
| Core competencies | 3–5 capabilities, in the customer's vocabulary |
| Differentiators | Why this company over others, with evidence |
| Past performance | Customers and contracts, with values where releasable |
| Company data | UEI, CAGE, NAICS, PSC codes, size and socioeconomic status, contract vehicles held |
| Contact | One named person, email and phone |

Tailor it to each agency. A generic statement is easy to ignore.
